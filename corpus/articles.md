# Articles: a, an, the, and the zero article

## a vs an

The choice follows the **sound** the next word starts with, not the letter:
*an hour, an honest mistake, an MBA* (sounds: /aʊ/, /ɒ/, /ɛm/) but *a university,
a European city, a one-way street, a unit* (sounds: /juː/, /wʌ/).

## Indefinite (a/an) vs definite (the)

Use **a/an** for a first mention or any member of a class: *I bought **a** book.*
Use **the** when the listener can identify which one — because it was mentioned
before (*The book was expensive*), because it is unique (*the sun, the manager
of this branch*), because a modifier makes it specific (*the book **on the
table***), or because the context makes it obvious (*Close the door*).

## The zero article (no article)

Use no article with:

- Plural and uncountable nouns used generally: *Cats are independent.*
  *Water freezes at 0 °C.* *She studies economics.*
- Most proper names: *France, Paris, Mount Everest, Lake Geneva, Oxford
  University.*
- Meals, most transport, and institutions used for their purpose: *have
  breakfast, go by train, go to school / hospital / church / bed / prison.*
  Compare *go to **the** school* = the building, not the activity.

## The with names — the exceptions worth memorising

*the United States, the Netherlands, the Philippines* (plural or state-type
names), *the Nile, the Pacific, the Alps, the Middle East, the Sahara* (rivers,
seas, mountain ranges, regions, deserts), *the University of Oxford* (the X of
Y pattern), *the BBC, the UN* (organisations), *the Hilton* (hotels).

## Common L2 errors

*I am student.* → *I am **a** student.* (English requires an article before a
singular countable noun.)
*I like the music.* → *I like music.* (general, not a specific piece)
*The life is hard.* → *Life is hard.*
*She plays the piano* keeps *the*; *She plays football* does not.
