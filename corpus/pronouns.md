# Pronouns: case, agreement and reference

## Subject vs object case

Subject pronouns: *I, you, he, she, it, we, they.*
Object pronouns: *me, you, him, her, it, us, them.*

The test for a compound is to remove the other person: *He and **I** went* (not
*me went*); *She invited **him** and **me*** (not *he and I*). *Between you and
**me*** is always object case — *between you and I* is a hypercorrection.

After *than* and *as*, either case is defensible, and they mean different
things: *She likes him more than **I** (do)* vs *…more than **him** (than she
likes him)*.

A pronoun after the verb *be* is formally subject case (*It is **I***), but
*It's me* is standard in every register below the ceremonial.

## who vs whom

*Who* is the subject; *whom* is the object of a verb or a preposition. The test:
answer the question with *he* or *him* — if *him* fits, use *whom*.
*__Who__ called?* (he called) · *__Whom__ did you call?* (you called him) ·
*To __whom__ should I send it?* (send it to him).
In defining relative clauses the same test applies: *the manager **who**
approved it* (he approved it) · *the manager **whom** we met* (we met him).
*Whom* is disappearing from speech; after a preposition it is still expected in
writing.

## whose vs who's

*Whose* is possessive (*the writer **whose** book won*); *who's* = *who is* or
*who has*.

## Agreement with the antecedent

A pronoun matches the noun it refers to in number: *Every **student** must bring
**their** own laptop* — singular *they* is now standard for a person of unknown
or unstated gender, and avoids the clumsy *his or her*. What it cannot do is
switch mid-sentence: ✗ *A student should bring their laptop, and he must sign
in.*

## Reflexives

*Myself, yourself, himself…* are for when the subject and object are the same
person (*I taught **myself**.*) or for emphasis (*The director **herself**
called*). They are not polite substitutes for *I* or *me*: ✗ *Please contact
myself* → *Please contact **me**.*

## Ambiguous reference

Every *it, this, they* must have exactly one possible antecedent. ✗ *When the
laptop touched the desk, **it** was scratched.* Name the thing instead.
